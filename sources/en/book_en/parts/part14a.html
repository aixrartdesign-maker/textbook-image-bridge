  • thin uniform line weight

  • no text, no lettering

  • neutral silhouette figure

RatioLandscape diagrams 16:9, people comparisons 4:3

  • aspect ratio 16:9 or 4:3

Cautions when making plates

  • Text inside generated images usually comes out garbled. Add any text yourself at the editing stage.
  • For plates with people, ask for silhouettes or neutral forms so they don't look like real people.
  • Don't ask for frames from specific films or ads to be recreated. That breaches both copyright and ethics.
  • Check the tool's terms for commercial use, and review the license scope before printing and distribution.
  • For print, work out the resolution needed at 300dpi, then upscale.

References and sources

The figures and standards in the text draw on public sources in the categories below and on industry practice.

On sources

Technical specs and platform policies change often. Always check the latest official documents before production and delivery. Work by the directors and studios cited is mentioned only to analyze technique, and no frames have been reproduced.

Theory and historyFrom perception to lighting manuals

  • Classic psychology literature on apparent motion and the phi phenomenon
  • Film history surveys and introductions to film theory (montage theory, classical editing grammar)
  • Translations of original Soviet montage texts (Kuleshov effect, collision montage)
  • Cinematography and lighting manuals (3-point lighting, lighting ratios, portrait lighting patterns)

Technical standardsStandards and manufacturer documents

  • International broadcast and cinema standards (resolution, frame rate, color space)
  • Camera makers' official manuals and white papers
  • Official Adobe Premiere Pro and After Effects help
  • Official Blackmagic Design DaVinci Resolve manual
  • LED display makers' spec sheets (pixel pitch, luminance, refresh rate)

Platform guidesEach service's official guidance

  • YouTube Creator Academy and official Help Center guides
  • Ad spec documents in the Meta Business Help Center

  • Naver's official search and blog guides

Types of learning resources and how to use them

Official docsThe most accurate source

  • Knowing exact feature names makes searching far more efficient

Manufacturer materialsThorough on principles

  • Camera · lighting white papers and manuals, free and highly reliable

Video tutorialsFollowing along isn't enough

  • After watching, always remake it with different material

Global ad award archivesEfficient for learning structure

  • Read the winners' case summaries alongside

Theory booksOne book, many times

  • Film theory and editing manuals are better read repeatedly

CommunitiesRead processes, not just questions

  • You learn more from how others solve problems
