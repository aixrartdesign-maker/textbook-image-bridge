2.1
Feature · STORYBOARD

The storyboard,
a promise made in pictures

A storyboard is a matter of information design, not drawing skill. This feature sets out in one place what a panel must say for ten people to shoot the same scene, and how the panel changes across media: horizontal · vertical · ultra-wide · 360 · curved · facade · AR/VR/MR/XR.

01ContinuityThe director sorting out ideas. Rough drawings, several versions compared, for internal meetings. 1–2 hours.
02StoryboardThe blueprint for the whole crew. Exact framing · timing · sound. Minimal changes after PPM approval. 1–2 days.
03AnimaticThe boards on a timeline with temp music · narration. Rhythm and length problems show at once. 2–3 days.
TIP

For a 15-second ad or a 1-minute short-form piece, go all the way to an animatic. Rhythm problems do not show in still pictures.

The storyboard is the last point in the production pipeline where mistakes are cheap to fix. If a change at the concept stage costs 1, it costs 3 at the board stage, 20 on shoot day, and 50 in post. So a board is not a document for drawing nicely but a document for getting it wrong fast.

SPREAD 01Anatomy of a panel — all nine items, or no one else can shoot it

Inside the panelOnly what is seen

  • Position and size of people · objects (shot size is drawing size)
  • Gaze direction, movement arrows (subject = black, camera = wine)
  • Caption · logo areas entering the frame as dotted boxes
  • Safe-area dotted line — captions only inside it

Outside the panelWhat is heard, and measurements

  • Time: both duration in seconds and running time code
  • Abbreviations: SIZE / ANGLE / LENS / MOVE / TR
  • Action notes as verbs, emotions as adjectives, kept apart
  • N · V.O. · SYNC · M · SE — four layers of sound on one line

SPREAD 03Horizontal and vertical are not different media but different languages — design the same cut twice

Vertical board sample — 15-second short-form ad (9:16, 6 cuts)

A vertical board still reads with up to 6 panels per row. Vertical panels are narrow, so information runs long beneath them; write one line of action note, one word of sound. Draw captions only in the middle band, clear of the UI areas (gray).

SPREAD 04360 · VR boards — design an 'axis of attention,' not a timeline

A board for flat video records "what is seen in this cut." A 360 board records where the audience will be looking and what will make them turn. The panel is a 2:1 equirectangular unwrap, always drawn with a top view above it.

360 videoThe audience turns around

  • Camera locked, eye height 1.2–1.6m
  • Long cuts (8 s+), fade transitions
  • Near subjects at least 1.5m away — stereo distortion

VR (headset)The audience stands in the scene

  • FOV 90–110°; 2–5m in front of the eyes is the main stage
  • No head-locked UI · captions; attach them to the world
  • Motion-sickness rule: no camera moves, or constant-speed straight lines only

Board deliverablesA set of three sheets

  • Equirectangular unwrap (panels)
  • Top view (angles · movement)
  • Attention timeline (seconds × front-angle graph)

SPREAD 06AR · MR · XR boards — the panel splits into 'layers'

An AR · MR panel is not one flat drawing; it breaks down into reality layer / tracked surface / virtual object layer / UI layer. And "what the audience must do to reach the next panel (interaction)" replaces the transition mark. An XR stage (LED-wall virtual production) works the other way: the board splits into four tracks of camera · LED background · physical set · lighting.

SPREAD 08Making narration and sound 'visible' — the 3-lane board and a finished 15-second ad

Sound is the information most often missing from a board. Written as one line under the picture, its length is invisible. Lay picture lane / voice lane / sound lane side by side on a timeline, and you can see, as if with a ruler, whether the narration runs longer than the cut and whether music changes land on the cuts.

SPREAD 09Drawing for legibility — the set is dark and the reader is busy

  1. 01Draw in three tones. Background (light), midground (medium), people/product (dark). Three values show form; five make a picture — a board is not a picture.
  2. 02Only two line weights. Thick for outlines, thin for detail. If every line is the same weight, nothing comes forward.
  3. 03One color for arrows; black for character movement. Camera moves in one color, wine (or red). Past three colors, people start hunting for the legend.
  4. 04Cut number and duration are the largest text in the panel. The words shouted most on set are "which cut, how many seconds."
  5. 05No text inside the drawing. Mark captions and logos with a dotted box only. All text goes below the panel.
  6. 06Two lines of info below the panel. Line one: measurements (SIZE · ANGLE · LENS · MOVE). Line two: words and sound (N · SYNC · M · SE). Nobody reads a third line.
  7. 07Text at least 11pt (on A4), 1/40 of panel width. Draw assuming it will be blown up to A3 and pinned.
  8. 08Figure size in frame = shot size. If the figure size changes between panels labeled MS, the board is lying. Make a silhouette ruler and use it.
  9. 093–6 panels per row, left→right, top→bottom. Zigzag or vertical layouts always get read out of order on set.
  10. 10The legend takes the first panel. Aspect ratio, resolution, symbol key, version and date go in panel one. The moment two board versions circulate, the shoot is lost.

Good acting directions

  • Separate action (verbs) from emotion (adjectives)
  • Write the behavior, not the result
  • Always state eyeline direction
  • e.g. Lift the product and shift eyes to camera
  • e.g. One breath in, then raise only the corners of the mouth

Bad acting directions

  • Look happy (no clear criterion)
  • Be natural (the most irresponsible direction)
  • Don't be nervous (negative direction)
  • More feeling (nobody knows what that means)
  • Just do your thing (abdicating direction)

NOTE

Korean narration runs about 3 characters per second. The caps: 35–45 characters for a whole 15-second ad, 150–180 for a 1-minute video.

Do not make captions and narration identical. Captions carry key words only; narration is written in sentences. Design for muted viewing and keep captions inside the safe area. Symbols must be uniform within the team, with the legend in the first panel.

Exercise2.1
Task
Storyboard a 30-second video in 8 or more panels, in two versions: 16:9 and 9:16.
Tip
Shot size, direction and movement arrows matter more than drawing skill. A vertical board normally needs more cuts.
Assessment
Flow between panels, camera notation, composition differences between the two ratios
Model answer
Photograph the board, string the panels into an animatic and verify the timing.
Exercise2.1
Task
Build a template with all nine elements of a panel (number · time · picture · size/angle · move · action note · dialogue · sound · transition) filled in, then design one extra panel for a 360 or AR version.
Tip
Set text size and panel layout for the person who will read it on set. Give the 360 panel a viewpoint diagram too.
Assessment
Completeness of information, usability on set, fit to the medium
Model answer
Test it: can a teammate read it and shoot without any explanation?
Exercise2.1
Task
Draw one storyboard page in three lanes (picture · words · sound) using every symbol: pan, zoom, dolly, track and cut transitions.
Tip
Symbols must be uniform within the team. Put the legend in the first panel. Convert narration length at 3 characters per second to set lane widths.
Assessment
Accuracy of symbol use, alignment of sound with cut boundaries
Model answer
Clear enough that the camera moves can be reproduced from the symbols alone.
